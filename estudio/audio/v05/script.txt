Train wheels aren't really wheels. They're cones. Look closely at any train. The wheels are wider inside and narrower outside. Like two cones, joined by one steel axle. Both wheels are locked together. They always spin at the same speed. So how does a train turn a corner? Plot twist. The real reason is the cone. In a curve, the train drifts outward. The outer wheel rolls on its fat part. The inner wheel rolls on its thin part. Bigger circle, more distance per turn. So the outer wheel goes farther, without slipping. Cars need a gearbox called a differential for that. Trains just use shape. And on straight track, the cones keep it centered too. So next time you see a train... look for the cones.
