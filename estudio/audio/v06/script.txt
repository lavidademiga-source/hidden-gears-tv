Your microwave door is full of holes. On purpose. Look through the window. That's a metal mesh, packed with tiny holes. About 2 mm each. Inside, the oven fires waves that heat your food. So why don't they leak out? Plot twist. The real reason is size. Microwaves are huge waves. About 12 cm long. Almost 5 inches. A 2 mm hole is way too small for them. So the metal mesh bounces most of them back inside. Engineers keep the holes far smaller than the wave. One classic design rule says under a tenth of it. But light waves are tiny. Thousands of times smaller than those holes. So light slips right through, and you can watch your popcorn. Same door. Two kinds of waves. One stays in... and one gets out.
