Every QR code has three big squares. And they're not decoration. Look at any QR code. Three corners have a square inside a square. The fourth corner doesn't. Plot twist. The real reason is direction. Those squares tell your camera three things. Number three: where the code is. Number two: how big it looks. Number one: which way is up. Only three corners have them, so the missing one gives it away. That's why you can scan it sideways, upside down, or at an angle. The squares use a rare black and white pattern. Printed text almost never matches it. QR codes were invented in 1994, to track car parts in a factory. So next time you scan one... thank the three squares.
