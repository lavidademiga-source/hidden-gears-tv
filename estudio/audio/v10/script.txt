California roads have about 20 million bumps. On purpose. Look between the lanes. Little round dots, glued to the road. Drive over them, and your car rumbles. They're called Botts' dots. Named after Elbert Botts, a California highway researcher. His team started testing them in the 1950s. Engineers may have studied the idea as early as 1936. Plot twist. The real reason started with paint. Painted lines kept wearing off, and they vanished in the rain. Raised dots stayed visible. And you could feel them. Drift out of your lane... and the rumble wakes you up. But now California is phasing them out. They're costly to keep replacing. And newer reflective paint does the job. So if you hear that rumble... you're driving over history.
