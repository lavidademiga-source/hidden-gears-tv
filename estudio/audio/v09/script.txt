Why do plug prongs have holes? Grab any lamp plug. Two flat prongs. Two tiny holes. The internet has three answers. Myth one: they save metal. There's no proof of that. The holes are tiny. Myth two: they lock into the outlet. Half true. Some early outlets had little bumps inside. The holes clicked onto them. But today, most outlets just grip the prongs by friction. Plot twist. The real reason today is the factory. The US plug standard calls the hole optional. And it says the hole is for manufacturing. Machines hold the prongs by those holes. Then the plastic is molded around them. Bonus: a small lock or warning tag fits through them. So that tiny hole... is mostly a handle for machines.
