What if the most important hole on a padlock isn't for the key? Flip one over. There's a tiny second hole, and no key fits it. It looks like a mistake. Or a secret keyhole. Plot twist. The real reason is weather. It does three jobs. Let's count down. Number three: oil. Some lock makers say a drop of lubricant can go in here. Number two: ice. Padlocks live outside, in rain and snow. Water trapped inside can freeze and jam the lock. Number one: drainage. Rain sneaks in through the keyhole. Gravity pulls it down, and out through that tiny hole. No trapped water means less rust, and no frozen lock. So the hole that matters most... isn't the one for the key.
